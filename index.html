    How big the lyrics are (the piano roll gets the rest of the space). You can also drag the line between the lyrics and the piano roll; double-click it for 100%.

    A dark box behind the lyrics, like video captions, so they read over any video. In background mode the box is always on, at least 60% opaque.

    Video

    Ambient glow: the bars around the video glow softly with its colours (like YouTube's ambient mode) — also black bars that are part of the video itself, which are found by watching the edges that never show anything. Black: plain black bars.

    Whole video: the picture fits on the screen behind everything (under the controls and the visualizer), with bars where its shape differs from the screen's. Beside the controls: the picture fits in the space the controls, the track tabs, the visualizer and the lyrics bar leave free — nothing is on it — and they all sit on the bars around it. (The full-screen piano roll still draws over it; its Bar layout doesn't.) Fill the screen: no bars, but the edges that don't fit are cut off.

    Playback

    Precise: every track is decoded into memory and all of them play from one clock, so they can never drift apart; the whole mix follows the video by playing a tiny bit faster or slower. Standard: one player per track (uses less memory); each track corrects itself when it is quiet. Auto picks Precise when the device has enough memory.

    Files are read completely before they play, and nothing starts until everything is ready. Turn off if the app runs out of memory with very large files.

    Turns off all syncing — between the tracks and with the video — so every track can get its own speed: a Speed slider appears on each track. The tracks then drift apart on purpose; Sync (S) lines them all up again where you are. Turning this off puts every track back to normal speed, in sync.

    Debug

    Remove all files takes away what you added — every audio track, the video and the score — and nothing else: your settings, saved projects and playlist stay as they are.

    Sync

    Play the song and move whatever is off while you watch and listen: + is later, − is earlier. Save the project to keep it.

    Open a song to line it up.

    Drag a bar left or right, or use the numbers below: − and + move one by the step beside them, and a click on the step changes it. What falls left of the song's start is cut off.

    These go with the project once it's saved: Projects → Save.

    This device

    Speaker delay (ms)

    How much later the sound reaches you than this device makes it — Bluetooth headphones, or AirPlay chosen on an iPhone, iPad or Mac (about 2000). The picture (visualizer, piano roll, lyrics, video) waits that long, so it stays in time with what you hear. The desktop app's own AirPlay allows for its delay by itself.

    Auto: on computers the video follows the audio, so the music never speeds up, slows down or jumps to stay with the picture; on phones and tablets the audio follows the video, which copes better when their video playback stutters.

    Projects

    Save the current audio, video, score (SVP / MIDI / VoiSona / UST / USTX / VSQX) and all settings as a project, and reopen it any time. Files are copied into the app, so projects keep working even if the originals move.

      Export makes one .mapproj file with the project and all its files (audio, video, score) — keep it as a backup, or open it on another computer or phone with Import (or by dropping it on the player). Importing never replaces a project: one with the same name comes in as “… (2)”. Export current… makes one of what's loaded right now, as it is at this moment, whether or not it's saved as a project: nothing is saved and no project changes.

      Background mode

      Plays your saved projects one after another, as a playlist — the next song is loaded while the current one plays, so there's no waiting in between. In the desktop app it plays in a small player in the corner of the screen, on top of your other windows — drag it by its top edge, resize it by its edges; the ↗ button (or Esc) takes you back to the full player, with nothing loaded.

      Playlist

        To change the order, drag a song up or down: with a mouse anywhere on its row, on a touch screen by the dots at its left. With the keyboard: Alt+↑ or Alt+↓ on a song.

        Visuals only: just the visualizer and the piano roll with its lyrics — no video, no backgrounds.

        In the small player (the corner window), only the main singer's lines — or, while the main singer is quiet, the line that's being sung right now. In all of background mode, the lyrics and the syllables on the notes sit on a dark caption box, and so does the time, so they stay easy to read over anything behind them; in the small player and the browser's compact player the song name and "Next" do too (on phones they stay in the bar at the top). Its opacity is the song's Caption box opacity, at least 60%.

        Install the newest version to get Desktop pet mode (this copy of the app is from before it).

        Desktop pet: the singers stand on your taskbar and sing their lines in speech bubbles; the first of them to sing is there as the song starts, and each of the others comes on just before their first line. Only the singers and their bubbles show — the mouse goes straight through everywhere else. Drag a singer to move it (where you leave it is remembered); click one to see the song's name. Ctrl+Alt+H hides and shows them while the music goes on; Ctrl+Alt+End (or ✕ on the little controls at the top of the screen, in the middle) stops. Each singer is a picture of your own: Characters… lists the singers of the songs in your playlist, so you can give them their pictures before you start, and warns about any without one. When you start, the player also asks for one for every singer in the playlist that doesn't have one yet, unless you left it out or removed its picture before (Characters… still gives it one). Only singers with a picture come on — a singer you leave out isn't shown.

        Drag a singer up off the taskbar and it stays where you leave it, in the air; let it go near the taskbar and it stands on it again. Off: the singers stay on the taskbar and only move left and right.

        Let go of a singer just over the top edge of a window and it stands on it: it rides along when you move the window, and drops to the taskbar when the window closes, is minimised or gets covered there.

        With two or more singers on screen, one that has no line for a while strolls along the taskbar (or the window it stands on) and is standing still again before its next line. It never walks into the others, it stays put for a while after you move it, and the place you gave it is still the one that's remembered.

        Singers who never sing at the same time share one pet, which sings all their lines; singers who do sing together stay separate pets. The pet is the one of them with a picture who sings most. A singer without a picture joins a pet when it can, so its lines show too.

        The singers of the songs in the playlist above: only singers with a picture come on. Choose picture… or Change… gives one its picture (the pets on screen take it at once); Remove takes it away — that singer isn't shown any more. The list goes song by song, in the playlist's order. To change several singers at once, select their cards (click one; Ctrl+click adds one or takes it out; Shift+click takes all in between), then Choose picture… on any of them: one file goes to all of them, several files are handed out down the list, in the order of their names.

        Voicebank pictures gives each singer the picture that comes with its voicebank, if that voicebank is installed on this computer (OpenUtau, UTAU, Synthesizer V, VOCALOID or VoiSona). Singers who have a picture keep it unless you press again; with cards selected, only those change.

          Cover the screen: instead of the small window, the visualizer and the piano roll play over your whole screen, with nothing behind them — your other windows show through, and the mouse goes straight through to them. The player waits in the taskbar as the remote control. The controls are in a little window in the bottom-right corner of the screen (the song, ⏮ ⏯ ⏭, and ✕ to stop), and these keys work from any app while it plays: Ctrl+Alt+Space play / pause, Ctrl+Alt+Page Down next song, Ctrl+Alt+Page Up previous song, Ctrl+Alt+End stop — and the media keys on your keyboard.
