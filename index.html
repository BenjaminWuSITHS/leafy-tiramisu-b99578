    Settings

    Appearance

    A soft glow from what's playing: from the visualizer without a video; with a video, the controls pick up the picture's colours. (The glow in the video's bars: Settings → Video → Video bars.)

    How far the visualizer moves: lower it if it keeps hitting the top, raise it if it barely moves. 100% is the middle.

    Customization

    Track colours

    Pick a colour to give one track its own.

    Score SVP / MIDI / VoiSona / UST / USTX / VSQX

    How big the lyrics are (the piano roll gets the rest of the space). You can also drag the line between the lyrics and the piano roll; double-click it for 100%.

    A dark box behind the lyrics, like video captions, so they read over any video. In background mode the box is always on, at least 60% opaque.

    Video

    Ambient glow: the bars around the video glow softly with its colours (like YouTube's ambient mode) — also black bars that are part of the video itself, which are found by watching the edges that never show anything. Black: plain black bars.

    Whole video: the picture fits on the screen behind everything (under the controls and the visualizer), with bars where its shape differs from the screen's. Beside the controls: the picture fits in the space the controls, the track tabs, the visualizer and the lyrics bar leave free — nothing is on it — and they all sit on the bars around it. (The full-screen piano roll still draws over it; its Bar layout doesn't.) Fill the screen: no bars, but the edges that don't fit are cut off.

    Auto: on computers the video follows the audio, so the music never speeds up, slows down or jumps to stay with the picture; on phones and tablets the audio follows the video, which copes better when their video playback stutters.

    Playback

    Starts all the audio this much later in the song: the video and the piano roll play their first seconds before the music comes in (e.g. a music video with an intro the audio files don't have).

    Cuts this much off the beginning of every audio track: the song starts that far into the audio, the video and the piano roll stay where they are (e.g. audio files with a count-in or silence the video doesn't have). Works together with Audio starts at: the cut audio comes in then.

    Precise: every track is decoded into memory and all of them play from one clock, so they can never drift apart; the whole mix follows the video by playing a tiny bit faster or slower. Standard: one player per track (uses less memory); each track corrects itself when it is quiet. Auto picks Precise when the device has enough memory.

    Files are read completely before they play, and nothing starts until everything is ready. Turn off if the app runs out of memory with very large files.

    How much later the sound reaches you than this device makes it — Bluetooth headphones, or AirPlay chosen on an iPhone, iPad or Mac (about 2000). The picture (visualizer, piano roll, lyrics, video) waits that long, so it stays in time with what you hear. The desktop app's own AirPlay allows for its delay by itself.

    Turns off all syncing — between the tracks and with the video — so every track can get its own speed: a Speed slider appears on each track. The tracks then drift apart on purpose; Sync (S) lines them all up again where you are. Turning this off puts every track back to normal speed, in sync.

    Warnings

    App updates

    Checking for updates…

    The repository is private, so the app needs a GitHub token to see new versions. On github.com: your picture → Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Tokens (classic) → Generate new token (classic), tick repo, generate, and paste it here. It is stored encrypted on this computer and only ever sent to GitHub.

    New versions come from the main branch on GitHub. One is downloaded by itself and goes in straight away when nothing is open in the player; otherwise when you click Restart, or the next time the app starts.

    Discord

    Like Spotify: while something plays, your Discord profile shows “Listening to …” with the song (the project's name, or the first file's), its place in the playlist, and a time bar. It needs the Discord app open on this computer; nothing shows while the player is paused.

    Once: on discord.com/developers/applications → New Application, and give it the name your friends should see (e.g. Multi Audio Player). Copy its Application ID (General Information) and paste it here. For a picture: Rich Presence → Art Assets, add an image named icon.

    Debug

    Projects

    Save the current audio, video, score (SVP / MIDI / VoiSona / UST / USTX / VSQX) and all settings as a project, and reopen it any time. Files are copied into the app, so projects keep working even if the originals move.

      Export makes one .mapproj file with the project and all its files (audio, video, score) — keep it as a backup, or open it on another computer or phone with Import (or by dropping it on the player). Importing never replaces a project: one with the same name comes in as “… (2)”.

      Background mode

      Plays your saved projects one after another, as a playlist — the next song is loaded while the current one plays, so there's no waiting in between. In the desktop app it plays in a small player in the corner of the screen, on top of your other windows — drag it by its top edge, resize it by its edges; the ↗ button (or Esc) takes you back to the full player, with nothing loaded.

      Playlist

        Visuals only: just the visualizer and the piano roll with its lyrics — no video, no backgrounds.

        In the small player (the corner window), only the main singer's lines — or, while the main singer is quiet, the line that's being sung right now. In all of background mode, the lyrics and the syllables on the notes sit on a dark caption box, and so does the time, so they stay easy to read over anything behind them; in the small player and the browser's compact player the song name and "Next" do too (on phones they stay in the bar at the top). Its opacity is the song's Caption box opacity, at least 60%.

        Cover the screen: instead of the small window, the visualizer and the piano roll play over your whole screen, with nothing behind them — your other windows show through, and the mouse goes straight through to them. The player waits in the taskbar as the remote control. The controls are in a little window in the bottom-right corner of the screen (the song, ⏮ ⏯ ⏭, and ✕ to stop), and these keys work from any app while it plays: Ctrl+Alt+Space play / pause, Ctrl+Alt+Page Down next song, Ctrl+Alt+Page Up previous song, Ctrl+Alt+End stop — and the media keys on your keyboard.

            Volume

            The whole mix plays on the device. It's heard there a moment later (the Delay above), and the picture here waits for it; pausing stops it on the device at once. If it stutters, or the picture runs ahead of the sound there, choose Steady. From an iPhone, iPad or Mac, Screen Mirroring puts the player itself — picture and sound — on an Apple TV.
